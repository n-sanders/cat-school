## Round shapes

Both of these shapes are **curved all the way around**, with no corners or straight
edges.

- A **circle** is equally round everywhere. Every point on its edge is the same
  distance from the center.
- An **ellipse** (an oval) looks like a circle that has been **stretched** in one
  direction.

**Circle or ellipse?** Compare the width and the height. If they are the same, it is a
circle.

## Counting carefully

Pentagons and hexagons look alike at a glance, so **count the sides**:

- A **pentagon** has **five** sides. It is often drawn like a simple house, with a

## Counting rule

Every polygon has **as many corners as it has sides**. A triangle has three of each, a
square four, and so on.

## Name prefixes

Many shape names start with a Greek or Latin number word:

| Prefix | Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tri- | three | triangle |
| penta- | five | pentagon |
| hexa- | six | hexagon |
| octa- | eight | octagon |

A **circle** is not a polygon, because a polygon must be made only of straight sides.

</details>
