## Why features matter

When experts identify a cat, they do not just glance at it and guess. They check a
few **features** one at a time, then put the clues together. Many breeds share a color,
so color alone is rarely enough.

## The five features to check first

1. **Coat** - Is the hair short, long, curly, or almost missing? Is it plush, silky,
   shaggy or sleek? What color and pattern is it?
2. **Face and head** - Is the face *flat*, with a short snub nose (Persian)? Is the
   head a long *wedge* (Siamese), or *round* with full cheeks (British Shorthair)?
3. **Ears** - Are they huge or small, upright or folded? Do they have tufts of fur at
   the tips?
4. **Eyes** - What shape are they (round or almond) and what color (blue, green,
   copper, gold)?
5. **Body** - Is the cat *cobby* (short, broad and solid) or *slender* (long and
   fine-boned)? How long are the legs and tail?

## Watch out

- **Kittens** often look different from adults - their coats and faces are not fully
  developed.
- **Random-bred cats** can look a lot like a breed without being one. Most pet cats
  have no breed at all.
- **Lighting** can make colors look lighter or darker than they really are.

Look at the example photos below and find each feature in the captions before you take
the quiz.

## Five patterns to know

The **pattern** of a coat is often the fastest clue to a breed.

- **Solid** - one color all over, like the black Bombay or the blue Russian Blue.
- **Tabby** - stripes, swirls or blotches on a lighter background. Most tabbies also
  have a dark **"M"** on the forehead.
- **Spotted** - separate dark spots instead of stripes, like the Egyptian Mau or Bengal.
- **Ticked** - each hair has bands of dark and light color. From a distance the coat
  looks like one warm, glowing color with **no spots or stripes** on the body. The
  Abyssinian is the classic ticked cat.
- **Colorpoint** - a pale body with darker **points**: the face mask, ears, legs and
  tail. Colorpoint cats almost always have **blue eyes**. The Siamese is the best-known
  example.

## Watch out

Pattern is a clue, not an answer. Plenty of ordinary random-bred cats are tabbies, and
several different breeds are colorpoint. Always check the coat length, face and body
too.

## The cat on the sidewalk

The photos in this course are **pedigreed** cats, from a breed with a known family
line. Every quiz has a breed as the answer.

Most cats you will meet are **random-bred**. They have no breed at all. Shelters
and clinics call them a **domestic shorthair** or a **domestic longhair**.

A **breed cross** is a different, much smaller group: a cat whose parents were
pedigreed, such as a Bengal and a Maine Coon. That is the cat version of a mixed
breed. It is not the usual house cat.

## How often is a cat pedigreed?

Purebred cats are a small share of pet cats. Purebred dogs are not.

- In a large study of British veterinary visits, about **10% of cats** were
  purebred, and about **84% of dogs** were.
- In a 1997 survey of US households, owners described about **14% of cats** and
  about **57% of dogs** as purebred.

Owner counts for cats run a little high, because a pointed or fluffy cat often
gets a breed name it does not have. Use this course when a cat is known to be
pedigreed, or when a photo is labeled with a breed. On the street, start with
domestic shorthair or domestic longhair.

## Looks that do not name a breed

These are ordinary in random-bred cats:

- **Tabby** stripes or swirls, including a silver tabby. A silver tabby is not
  an American Shorthair unless you know that it is.
- **Solid** black, gray, or orange.
- A **colorpoint** coat, pale with dark points and blue eyes. A pointed cat with
  no papers is not a Siamese.
- **Long, fluffy fur**, including a brown tabby. Fluff does not make a Maine Coon.

## Looks that almost always mean a breed

A few traits are rare unless the cat comes from a breed, or from a recent cross
with one:

- ears **folded** forward
- a short **curly** rex coat
- **hairless**, wrinkled skin
- a **flat face** with a snub nose
- **neat white gloves** on all four paws and no white anywhere else

Ordinary white paws are not that last clue. Lots of random-bred cats have white
paws together with a white chest, chin, or belly.

## One family, more than one name

You will meet several breeds that are one family in different coats. A Persian,
a Himalayan, and an Exotic Shorthair share a flat face. A Siamese and an Oriental
share a long, wedge-shaped head. Most cat breeds were developed in the twentieth
century, often by selecting a single coat or ear trait. A pedigreed cat still
belongs to a real breed. A cat that only looks similar usually does not.

</details>

## Three sturdy shorthairs

These breeds all have **short coats, sturdy bodies and round faces**. They are among
the most popular pedigree cats.

- **American Shorthair** - a strong, muscular working cat from North America. Its face
  is large with full cheeks, but a little **longer than it is wide**. The famous color
  is the **silver tabby**: black swirls on silver.
- **British Shorthair** - the "teddy bear" cat. Everything is **round**: round head,
  chubby cheeks, big round eyes and a short, broad body. The coat is **very dense
  and plush**. The classic color is **blue-gray**, and a blue cat's eyes are
  **copper or gold**. A silver tabby British Shorthair has green or hazel eyes.
- **Scottish Fold** - looks a lot like a British Shorthair, but its small ears **fold
  forward and down**, giving an owl-like face.

## Tips

- **Silver tabby with a slightly longer face?** Think American Shorthair.
- **Blue, very round and plush?** Think British Shorthair.
- **Ears flat against the head?** Think Scottish Fold. Some Scottish Folds are born
  with straight ears, though, so this clue only works one way.

## Two blue cats

- **Russian Blue** - slim, graceful and fine-boned, with long legs. The short blue
  coat has **silver tips** that make it shimmer. Its eyes are **vivid green**.
- **Chartreux** - a sturdy French cat with a broad body on comparatively short, fine
  legs. Its blue coat is dense and slightly **woolly**, and its eyes are **copper to
  orange**.

**Green eyes or orange eyes?** That is the fastest way to tell these two apart.

## Black and brown

- **Bombay** - a "mini panther": **solid glossy black** right to the roots, with
  **copper or gold eyes**. It was made by crossing sable Burmese with black American
  Shorthairs, so its body is compact and muscular like a Burmese.
- **Burmese** - a compact cat that is **heavier than it looks**, with a short,
  satin-like coat. The classic color is **sable**, a rich, warm dark brown. Its eyes
  are gold to yellow.

**Jet black or dark brown?** Look closely in good light: a Bombay is truly black.

## Three spotted cats

- **Bengal** - large and muscular, with **leopard-like rosettes** (two-toned spots) or
  marbling, often with a sparkly **"glitter"** sheen. It comes from crosses with the
  wild **Asian leopard cat**.
- **Egyptian Mau** - the only **naturally spotted** domestic breed. Its spots are
  random, on a silver, bronze or smoke coat. Look for **gooseberry-green eyes**, a
  slightly worried expression and dark "mascara" lines on the cheeks.
- **Ocicat** - named for the ocelot, but **all domestic** (Abyssinian and Siamese
  ancestors). It has big **thumbprint-shaped spots** in rows and an athletic build.

## One ticked cat

- **Abyssinian** - lithe and alert, with large ears. Every hair is **ticked** with
  bands of color, so the warm ruddy coat glows - but there are **no spots or stripes**
  on the body.

<details><summary>Lesson text</summary>

## Four blue-eyed colorpoints

All four breeds have a pale body, dark points and blue eyes. Work through three
questions.

1. **Is the coat short?** Then it is a **Siamese**.
2. **Is the face flat, with a snub nose?** Then it is a **Himalayan** - a colorpoint
   Persian.
3. **Is there white on the paws?**
   - **Neat white gloves on all four paws only** - **Birman**.
   - **White paws plus a white chin and belly stripe** (mitted), **lots of white with
     a white "V" on the face** (bicolor), or **mostly white with color only on the
     ears, tail, and mask** (van) - **Ragdoll**.
   - **No white at all?** It could be a colorpoint Ragdoll. Ragdolls are larger and
     heavier, with a normal-length muzzle.

The breed standards for the Siamese and Himalayan do not allow white paws, so white on
the feet points away from them.
